# Product Concept: AI Legal Filing Platform for Uzbekistan

## 1. Core Positioning

We are building an AI-powered legal filing platform that helps citizens and small businesses correctly prepare, validate, and submit legal applications to courts and government systems.

The product is not a generic legal chatbot and not an “AI lawyer.” It is a structured platform that turns a real-life legal problem into the right legal route, the right application type, the right documents, and a court-ready submission.

The core idea is simple:

> People should not lose access to justice because they selected the wrong form, missed a required document, wrote the application incorrectly, or did not know where to file.

Our platform helps users move from “I have a problem” to “I have a correctly prepared legal application.”

---

## 2. One-Line Description

An AI-powered platform that helps people understand their legal situation, choose the correct filing path, prepare court-ready documents, validate mistakes, and submit applications through official legal systems.

---

## 3. Short Pitch

Today, legal services are becoming digital, but the process is still too complex for ordinary people. Citizens can access online court portals, but they often do not know which form to choose, how to describe their case, what documents to attach, or where to submit the application. As a result, many applications are rejected because of simple procedural mistakes.

We are building an AI legal filing platform that guides users from a real-world problem to a correctly prepared legal application. The system asks the right questions, classifies the case, selects the correct filing path, prepares the application, checks for missing information, and helps the user submit it.

This improves access to justice for citizens, reduces manual burden for courts, and creates a scalable legal infrastructure layer for Uzbekistan’s digital justice system.

---

## 4. Strong Founder Framing

Digital courts solved the access problem at the infrastructure level, but not at the user level.

People can technically submit applications online, but they still need to understand legal categories, jurisdiction, procedural requirements, document rules, and form logic. Most citizens do not have that knowledge.

So the bottleneck is no longer only “can I access the system?”
The bottleneck is:

> “Can I understand what I need to submit, where to submit it, and how to submit it correctly?”

We solve this bottleneck.

Our product acts as the missing user-facing intelligence layer between citizens and the digital justice infrastructure.

## 5. Problem

Citizens and small businesses face several problems when trying to file legal applications:

### 5.1. They do not know the correct legal route

A person may have a valid legal issue, but they do not know whether it is a labor dispute, civil claim, consumer protection issue, fraud-related case, administrative complaint, or another category.

If the user chooses the wrong path, the application may be rejected or delayed.

### 5.2. They do not know which form to choose

Court and government portals often require the user to pick from many different forms and categories. To an ordinary user, these options are confusing.

The user may understand their problem emotionally, but not legally.

### 5.3. They do not know what information is required

Many applications fail because the user misses key details:

* incorrect applicant information;
* wrong respondent information;
* missing dates;
* missing amount of claim;
* incomplete explanation;
* no evidence attached;
* no contract or proof of payment;
* no employer/company details;
* wrong jurisdiction;
* wrong procedural category.

### 5.4. Rejection feedback is often not helpful enough

When an application is rejected, users often do not receive a clear, actionable explanation. They may not understand what exactly went wrong or how to fix it.

### 5.5. Legal help is too expensive for many people

Many people cannot afford a lawyer, especially for common but painful issues such as unpaid salary, consumer disputes, construction delays, small debts, or fraud.

Because of that, they either make mistakes or never file at all.

## 6. The Main Insight

The main problem is not just “people do not know the law.”

The deeper problem is:

> People do not know how to translate their life situation into the correct legal procedure.

They need more than legal information. They need procedural navigation.

They need help answering:

* What type of case is this?
* Where should I file?
* Which form should I use?
* What documents do I need?
* What should the application say?
* What mistakes can cause rejection?
* What should I do after submission?
* What happens if I get rejected?

That is the gap we are solving.

## 11. What the Product Does

### 11.1. Legal intake

The user describes the problem in normal language.

Example:

> “My employer has not paid my salary for two months.”

The system understands the situation and asks relevant follow-up questions.

### 11.2. Case classification

The platform classifies the case into the correct legal category.

Example:

* labor dispute;
* unpaid salary;
* employer as respondent;
* potential evidence required;
* possible claim amount;
* relevant filing route.

### 11.3. Filing route selection

The system helps choose the correct route:

* which body/court;
* which application type;
* which form;
* which documents;
* which procedural path.

### 11.4. Data collection

The platform collects structured information from the user:

* personal data;
* respondent data;
* dates;
* amounts;
* contracts;
* employment details;
* evidence;
* previous communication;
* rejection documents if any.

### 11.5. Document generation

The system generates a court-ready application draft.

Not just a generic text, but a structured document that follows the requirements of the selected case type.

### 11.6. Validation

Before submission, the system checks the application for common rejection risks:

* missing fields;
* wrong category;
* missing documents;
* unclear claims;
* inconsistent dates;
* insufficient evidence;
* wrong respondent;
* possible jurisdiction issue.

### 11.7. Filing guidance or submission

Depending on integration level, the platform can either:

* generate a ready-to-upload package;
* guide the user step by step through the official portal;
* or submit directly through an official integration.

For the hackathon MVP, guided filing and document export are enough.

### 11.8. Case workspace

Every user gets a case workspace:

* case summary;
* legal category;
* required documents;
* generated application;
* validation status;
* submission checklist;
* current status;
* next steps.

This makes the product feel like a real platform, not just chat.

## 12. Interface Direction

The product should not be only a chatbot.

A pure chatbot feels generic, uncontrolled, and hard to trust for legal workflows.

The product should be a hybrid interface:

### Chat for intake

Used when the user explains their problem and answers questions.

### Structured workspace for execution

Used for documents, forms, status, validation, and submission.

The ideal UX:

1. User starts with a simple question: “What happened?”
2. AI-guided intake asks questions.
3. A case workspace is created.
4. User sees their legal route.
5. User sees required documents.
6. User gets the generated application.
7. System runs a validation check.
8. User exports or submits.

This is much more serious than “talk to a legal bot.”
